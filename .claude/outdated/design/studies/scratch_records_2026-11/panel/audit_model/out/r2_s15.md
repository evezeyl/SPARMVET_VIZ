T1 (Year, Float64): [2001.0, 2002.0, 2002.5, 2002.5, 2003.0, 2002.0] with ids ['a', 'b', 'c', 'd', 'e', 'f']

T2 = step 1 `Year = pl.col('Year').floor()` (the explicit, visible lossy step), step 2 `polars_cast Year Int64` (now accepted): Year = [2001, 2002, 2002, 2002, 2003, 2002] (type Int64)
distinct values: T1 [2001.0, 2002.0, 2002.5, 2003.0]; T2 [2001, 2002, 2003] -> 3 values... 2002.0 and 2002.5 are two values at T1 and one at T2.

### The condition `Year = 2002` at each place
- at T1 (Float64), value 2002 read as 2002.0: selects ids ['b', 'f'] (2002.0 only; 2002.5 is another value)
- at T2 (Int64), value 2002: selects ids ['b', 'c', 'd', 'f'] (all four: the two rows that were 2002.5 are now 2002)
- two places, two row sets: T1 gives 2 rows (b, f), T2 gives 4 rows (b, c, d, f).

### Writing `Year != 2002` (remove 2002)
- only at T1: T1 ids ['a', 'c', 'd', 'e']; T2 ids ['a', 'c', 'd', 'e'] Year [2001, 2002, 2002, 2003]: the rows c and d (2002.5) are STILL there, and at T2 they read 2002. The same condition evaluated again at T2 finds 2 rows.
- only at T2: T2 ids ['a', 'e']; T1 unchanged (6 rows).
- at T1 AND at T2: T2 ids ['a', 'e']: the second step is NOT redundant (it removes c and d).
So the rule 'the earliest place covers the later ones' fails when a lossy step sits between: T1-only leaves the 2002.5 rows, which show as 2002 at T2.

### Detecting 'two values at T1 become one at T2'
- from the data of the two places only: distinct values T1 4, T2 3, same rows (6); 1 value(s) merged. Needs no row tracing. It is a count, valid when no row is removed in between.
- from the T1 distinct values and the step (the cast and the floor are readable from the recipe): the images: [(2001, 2001.0), (2002, 2002.0), (2002, 2002.5), (2003, 2003.0)]; images with more than one T1 value: [(2002, [2002.0, 2002.5])]
- from the data of both places and a unique key (id): join T1 and T2 on `id`: [(2001, [2001.0]), (2002, [2002.0, 2002.5]), (2003, [2003.0])] (value at T2 -> the values it had at T1). Needs a column that identifies a row at both places.
- cost on 2,000,000 rows (500,000 distinct at T1, 250,000 at T2): count distinct at one place 152 ms; both places 244 ms; join on the key and find the merged values 206 ms (250,000 values at T2 come from more than one value at T1).
