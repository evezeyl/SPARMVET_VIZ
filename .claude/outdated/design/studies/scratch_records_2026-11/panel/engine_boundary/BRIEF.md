## The questions
1. THE TARGET. Where does engine-neutral code live and where engine-dependent code? Give two or three variants of the boundary with pros and cons, and recommend one. For example: (V1) mark only: every file and text says which engine it belongs to, a test guards the imports, no code moves; (V2) an adapter per engine: each data engine and each plot engine provides a fixed set of services and everything else calls only those; (V3) V2 plus a neutral object crossing the seam (a table handle, a picture) so that the app never holds a LazyFrame or a plotnine object. For the recommended variant: the list of services an engine must provide (data: actions, types, formulas, filters and their missing-value meaning, join kinds, the plan of columns that the lineage walk needs, the readings of what a step writes, reading files, the stored format, hashing, error translation into plain words; plot: components, parameters, themes, fonts, palettes, seeds, the picture as bytes, error translation), and for each service where it lives today (FACTS) and where it would live.
2. WHAT A NEW ENGINE ADDS. With the recommended boundary, list exactly what is added (files, registry rows, texts, tests) for: a second plot engine in Python; the R backend of `r_backend.md`; a SQL or pandas data engine; a tier with another engine than its neighbours. And what would still have to CHANGE in existing code: be honest.
3. THE LINEAGE AND THE AUDIT. They read Polars action names and join kinds in `libs/utils`. Is "what a step writes" and "which joins can be followed" a service of the data engine? How is the walk kept neutral? What must the new work of today (the join warning, the uncertain drop, tables no plot reads) be marked with from the start?
4. THE RECIPE. A recipe part already says its engine by its block. Is that enough? Her view (ledger M216): a recipe for another engine has other names and tiers. What is neutral in the format and what is the engine's dialect; what the checker does.
5. THE SCIENCE (scientist and data scientist mostly). Same recipe intent, another engine: is the result the same? Types, missing values, row order, ties, floating point, random seeds, fonts. What must the app tell the user and record in the export. What can never be promised.
6. THE STANDING RULE AND ITS TEST. How a part is marked (module header, registry field, a list of allowed import places), the test that fails when Polars or plotnine is imported outside the allowed places or an engine-specific place is unmarked, and how user-facing texts that name an engine are handled (104 + 62 + 41 lines, 602 in docs).
7. HER TIME QUESTION, the main one. The rewrite that makes the app work with TODAY's two engines through the boundary (no second engine): how much work, in stages. For each stage: what it touches (files, from FACTS), whether it changes behaviour, how it is tested, its risk to the demo, and an honest size (in sessions of an agent builder, or small / medium / large with the count of files; say how sure). Which stages fit BEFORE the demo without risk, which are better AFTER. What of the work in flight should wait for which stage, and what can go on now because it is certainly neutral. What is lost by deciding fast and by waiting.
