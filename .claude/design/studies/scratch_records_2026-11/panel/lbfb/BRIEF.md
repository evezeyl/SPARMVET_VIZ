## The four questions she wants rechecked in the light of LBFB (she refused to answer them in a dialog "because I could not discuss")
1. **A rename across two tiers** (`a` to `b` in tier 1, `b` back to `a` in tier 2): one column followed through, named as it is at each tier's table; or two columns?
2. **What the reach counts**: every table whose stored table changes (also joins no plot reads: on the demo recipe the metadata table reaches 20 tables with them, 16 without, 13 plots); only tables a plot reads; or both said apart?
3. **A place in the reach where the column is gone** (it was not kept by a select, or turned into rows): a note under the line that reaches it; a grey line; or not shown?
4. **The drop of a key column**: the dialog opens with the key places grey and the reason; or one plain refusal before the dialog, as HOME does today?
(The other open questions of the first design are in `tmpAI/panel/lineage_tests/DESIGN.md` section 5: Q4, Q7, Q9, Q10, Q11 for wave 1; Q12 to Q28 later.)

## What each viewpoint must write (no word limit; complete, precise, no padding)
A. **What LBFB is EXPECTED to do**, as a walk-through on ONE concrete small recipe of your own making (tables, columns, steps, joins, two or three plots; small enough to hold in the head) and on the demo recipe's `MLST with metadata` (the join has steps at tier 1 and tier 2; two plots read it): move by move (back, forth, back again), what the walk knows at each move and from where (the map's edges; the engine's plan; the step arguments it may read), and the list of lines it returns.
B. **What it SHOULD do in the hard places**: for each of the four questions above: the behaviour you recommend under LBFB, what the walk must TRACK to do it (per column and per place: the name there, the type there, how it got there: unchanged, renamed, recomputed, cast, dropped, re-created; from which source column; through which steps), the alternatives, pros and cons, and one concrete example of each alternative going wrong.
C. **How it should be BUILT, in steps** (she says "maybe to solve this in several steps"): the smallest first step that is useful and testable on its own, then the next ones; for each step: what it adds, what it leaves grey, the tests that prove it (name the cases of DESIGN.md), and what the user would see if the dialog were built on that step alone. Say which steps need none of the open questions answered.
D. **What we will MISS**: edge cases not in DESIGN.md; things that should be tracked and are not; places where LBFB gives a confident wrong answer; what cannot be known and must be said to the user.
E. **Tests of the behaviour once it is built**: beyond the 90 cases: checks to run on the built mechanism against the real recipes (every column of every table of every shipped recipe as a start: the walk returns without a failure; the lines agree with an independent count), and what would count as "the LBFB works".
